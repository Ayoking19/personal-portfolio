The Problem Solver's Origin Story

Natural Marketing & The "Classroom Air Freshener"

My passion for marketing and problem-solving didn't start in a textbook; it started in primary school. My classmates had a habit of farting in class, and the smell was unbearable. Instead of just complaining, I went home, took my dad's empty aftershave bottle, and mixed soap, powder, body cream, and water to engineer my own air freshener. I ran **[User Testing: evaluating a product with real users to ensure it solves the problem without causing new ones]** to make sure the spray didn't make the smell worse. It worked perfectly. The class loved it, and the very next day, I sold my first custom bottle for 300 Naira. I've been identifying problems and building solutions ever since.

Scaling Solutions & Tech Translation

That entrepreneurial instinct only grew. In secondary school, I identified a **[Market Gap: an unfulfilled customer need within a specific demographic]** for cold drinking water, so I froze bottles overnight and sold them for a profit. In university, recognizing the late-night curfew blocked students from buying cold drinks, I rented a fridge, stocked it, and ran a mini-distribution hub. Today, as a Computer Science student, I use that exact same mindset. I know how to structure MySQL databases and tech architecture, but I absolutely despise writing software code. Instead, I use my CS knowledge as a translation tool to manage engineers, define product rules, and market solutions effectively.

Sports Data, Scouting & Gaming

Beyond marketing, my life is anchored by sports and gaming. As a deeply devoted Arsenal fan, I am glued to football. I understand deep tactical metrics and utilize tools like **[Wyscout: a premier professional football platform used for player scouting and match analysis]**, Microsoft Excel, and Power BI to evaluate talent based on strict criteria. When I'm not analyzing sports, I'm playing chess or testing video games. I have a meticulous eye for **[Quality Assurance: the process of identifying bugs and suggesting user-experience improvements]**, catching both massive flaws and the tiniest details that make a game fun.

The Culture: Friendly, Youthful & Authentic

I am trustworthy, friendly, and highly expressive. I believe the best work happens in fun, youthful, and low-stress environments. Whether I am writing football journalism, spotting game bugs, or marketing a new product, I don't get stressed when I am doing what I love. My philosophy is simple: identify the problem, build the solution, keep the team laughing, and never underestimate the power of a great plate of beans.
